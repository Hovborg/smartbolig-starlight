The meter's own port sends readings continuously, so you can see what a hob or a
heat pump draws while it happens. This guide takes you from the meter's port to
the Energy dashboard in Home Assistant.

You get a live power value and an energy counter that the Energy dashboard can
use. You need a meter whose port can be opened, a reader that Home Assistant can
talk to, and some patience with the grid company. Home Assistant must already be
running; see [Getting Started](/en/home-assistant/kom-godt-i-gang/) if not.

This guide covers Danish meters and Danish rules.

## What the HAN port is

The port has several names. The Green Power Denmark
[industry standard](https://greenpowerdenmark.dk/files/media/document/Branchestandard-for-fjernaflaeste-elmaaleres-eksterne-port.pdf)
lists H1 port, P1 port (Dutch) and HAN port (Norwegian) as names for the same
customer interface, and uses H1 itself. Radius, Flow Elnet and TREFOR write HAN
port, so we do too.

[Section 7 of the Danish meter order](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2023/477)
gives you the right to connect external devices to the remotely read meter using
open standards and to take out consumption data continuously. The transfer must
be protected by encryption or similar. A layperson must be able to make the
connection without breaking seals. The Danish Energy Agency writes in its
[interpretation of section 7](https://ens.dk/media/447/download) that the grid
company must hand out the keys that fit the software in the meter. The grid
company chooses the physical design and the specific standard. That is why your
meter may differ from the examples here.

The industry standard from February 2023 describes how the port should work on
meters bought from then on. The port only sends data out, at least every
10 seconds. It is deactivated from the start, the grid company opens it on
request, and the first activation must not cost the customer anything. The
standard does not necessarily apply to the meter you have today; read your grid
company's own terms. It also says the figures are statistical and are not
billing values.

<Aside type="caution" title="Never break a seal">
You may plug a reader into the HAN port yourself. You may not break seals, and
according to the Danish Safety Technology Authority you may not do electrical
work in the meter board or the consumer unit yourself. Follow the instructions
from the grid company and the supplier. If you are unsure which cover you may
remove, ask the grid company first. Work inside the board belongs with an
authorised electrical contractor.
</Aside>

## What four grid companies write

The procedure differs from company to company. This is what four grid companies
wrote on their own pages on 14 November 2026:

| Grid company | How you get access |
|---|---|
| [Radius Elnet](https://radiuselnet.dk/din-tilslutning/bestil-krypteringsnoegle/) | The meter is from Kamstrup. The HAN port sits under a cover, and you buy a HAN module that fits the meter yourself. You order one or two encryption keys and get them by email. |
| [Cerius](https://cerius.dk/din-tilslutning/bestil-krypteringsnoegle/) | You order the port opening and any encryption key in one go. Cerius writes that it is normally done within 30 minutes and is free of charge. |
| [Flow Elnet](https://flow-elnet.dk/hent-data-fra-din-maaler) | On newer meters the port is closed from the factory. Flow Elnet opens it or emails a code within 1 to 2 weeks. On Kamstrup 382M, 382L and 351 C the port is already open. |
| [TREFOR El-net](https://www.trefor.dk/kunde/elmaaler/) | Household meters can be replaced with a newer model with a HAN port. You pay a fee according to the company's price list. |

Other grid companies have their own rules, and we have not read them. Find your
own grid company's page before you buy anything. Meters that were in operation
before 3 December 2013 may stay in place under section 12 of the meter order,
even if they do not meet the requirements for the port.

## Choose a reader

| Solution | Suits | You maintain |
|---|---|---|
| Ready-made reader with a Home Assistant integration | You want it simple | The maker's firmware and support |
| ESP32 with ESPHome and an adapter that fits the meter | You want to build and control everything | Configuration file, key and ESPHome updates |
| Home Assistant's DSMR integration | Dutch, Belgian, Luxembourg and Swedish DSMR meters | Not documented for Danish meters |

Home Assistant's [DSMR integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/dsmr/)
does not mention Denmark, and it states that Swedish meters with a HAN port are
not supported. So check a ready-made reader's own documentation for your meter
type and your grid company before you buy it. We cannot know which readers work
for you.

The DIY route follows the ESPHome
[DLMS Meter](https://esphome.io/components/sensor/dlms_meter/) component. It only
listens and sends nothing to the meter. ESPHome shows specific setups for
Kamstrup Omnipower and Landis+Gyr E450, among others. ESPHome strongly
recommends an ESP32, because an ESP8266 is unsuitable for large packets. You
also need an adapter between the meter and the ESP, for example an M-Bus to UART
module. Which adapter fits depends on the meter's connector; ask the supplier
whether it fits your meter type.

## Step by step

<Steps>
1. **Find your meter type and your grid company**

   The grid company runs the grid and is responsible for the meter in your home.
   It is not the same as the electricity supplier you buy power from. The meter's type and number
   are printed on the meter. Find the grid company's page about access to meter
   data.

2. **Ask for the port to be opened and for keys**

   Use the grid company's form. The Radius form asks for the consumption number
   (aftagenummer) and the meter number, among other things. Ask the supplier of
   your module which key type to order; Radius distinguishes between one key and
   two keys for the DLMS/COSEM protocol. Prices and deadlines differ from
   company to company.

3. **Connect the reader**

   Fit the module following the supplier's instructions. Radius writes that you
   can usually plug a HAN module straight into the meter yourself. Break no
   seals. For ESPHome you connect the adapter's data output to a UART input on
   your ESP32.

4. **Configure the reader**

   ESPHome gives no baud rate or pin for Danish meters. It only says that the
   UART settings depend on your meter. Take them from the adapter's
   instructions. Keep the key in ESPHome's `secrets.yaml`, never in a public
   repository.

5. **Add the device in Home Assistant**

   Go to **Settings → Devices & services**. A discovered ESPHome device can be
   set up right away. Otherwise choose **Add integration → ESPHome** and enter
   the host name or IP address. See the
   [ESPHome integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/esphome/).

6. **Check the numbers against the meter display**

   Compare the energy counter in Home Assistant with the kWh figure on the
   meter display. If they differ by a fixed factor, for example 100 or 1000, the
   unit is wrong. Fix it before you go on; see the section on units below.

7. **Add the meter to Energy**

   Go to **Settings → Dashboards → Energy**. Under **Electricity grid** choose
   **Add grid connection**. Select the energy sensor for consumption from the
   grid and, if you have solar panels, the energy sensor for export. Select the
   counter in kWh or Wh here, not the power sensor. The rest is covered in
   [Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/).
</Steps>

### ESPHome example

The example follows ESPHome's own examples. Replace everything in capitals. The
pin and baud rate come from the adapter's instructions, and the keys come from
the grid company.

```yaml
uart:
  id: uart_dlms_bus
  rx_pin: GPIOXX
  baud_rate: BAUD_RATE_FROM_ADAPTER_INSTRUCTIONS

dlms_meter:
  id: dlms_meter_hub
  uart_id: uart_dlms_bus
  decryption_key: !secret han_decryption_key
  # auth_key: !secret han_auth_key   # only if your meter requires it

sensor:
  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.1.8.0.255"
    name: "Energy from grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "kWh"
    accuracy_decimals: 3
    device_class: energy
    state_class: total_increasing

  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.2.8.0.255"
    name: "Energy to grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "kWh"
    accuracy_decimals: 3
    device_class: energy
    state_class: total_increasing

  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.1.7.0.255"
    name: "Power from grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "W"
    device_class: power
    state_class: measurement

  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.2.7.0.255"
    name: "Power to grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "W"
    device_class: power
    state_class: measurement
```

The OBIS codes are the ones ESPHome itself lists: `1.0.1.8.0.255` is energy
taken from the grid, `1.0.2.8.0.255` is energy exported to the grid,
`1.0.1.7.0.255` is power from the grid, and `1.0.2.7.0.255` is power to the
grid. The industry standard's table has the same four quantities (1.8.0, 2.8.0,
1.7.0 and 2.7.0). The two "to grid" sensors only matter if you have solar
panels.

Your meter does not necessarily send every code. ESPHome's page shows how to
raise the log level to `VERBOSE` temporarily. The component then prints the
OBIS codes and values it can read to the log. Set the log level back afterwards.
ESPHome writes that a high log level can affect performance and use more
memory.

If you have a Kamstrup Omnipower, ESPHome shows a specific setup with an extra
`custom_patterns` section. Copy it from ESPHome's page. ESPHome has two key
fields, `decryption_key` and `auth_key`. If you received two keys, ask the grid
company or the supplier which is which.

### Units: check before you trust the number

ESPHome writes that `unit_of_measurement` is only a label and does no
conversion. ESPHome's example uses kWh for energy. The component's former
predefined keys used W for power and Wh for energy. The industry standard
specifies kW and kWh. So you cannot know in advance which unit your meter
sends.

Compare with the meter display. If the number does not match, change
`unit_of_measurement`, or add a
[`multiply` filter](https://esphome.io/components/sensor/) to the sensor until
the number matches the display.

ESPHome has deprecated the former predefined keys such as `voltage_l1` and the
`provider` option. They are removed in ESPHome 2026.11.0. Use `obis_code`.

## Test it

1. Open the new device in Home Assistant. Power should change continuously, and
   no sensor may show `unavailable`. The industry standard mentions at least
   every 10 seconds for new meters; your meter may send at a different rate.
2. Switch on a known load, for example a kettle. Power should rise by about the
   figure on the kettle's rating plate and fall again when you switch it off.
3. Note the counter in Home Assistant and on the meter display at the same
   moment. Wait an hour or a day, and compare the change. Small differences are
   normal, because the port's figures are statistical.
4. Under **Settings → Tools → Statistics**, check that the energy sensor has no
   issues. Home Assistant itself says it can take up to 2 hours before new data
   shows in Energy.

## When no data arrives

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing in the log | The port is still closed, or the module is not activated | Ask the grid company whether the opening is done |
| Packets arrive but values are missing | Wrong key, missing `auth_key`, or missing `custom_patterns` for your meter type | Check the key type, and compare with ESPHome's setup for your meter |
| Garbled or empty values | Wrong baud rate, parity or pin | Fix the UART using the adapter's instructions |
| Large packets are lost | ESP8266 or too small a buffer | Use an ESP32; ESPHome's UART page mentions `rx_buffer_size` for large payloads |
| The number is 100 or 1000 times too large or too small | The unit on the sensor does not match what the meter sends | Change `unit_of_measurement`, or use a `multiply` filter |
| The sensor cannot be chosen in Energy | `device_class`, `state_class` or unit is missing | Set them as in the example, and wait for statistics |
| Data suddenly stopped | The port may have been deactivated, for example at a change of customer | Ask the grid company to open the port again |

If consumption is doubled or negative in Energy, see
[troubleshooting in Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/).

## Undo

Remove the grid connection under **Settings → Dashboards → Energy**, delete the
ESPHome device under **Devices & services**, and take the reader out of the
port. History in Home Assistant is unchanged. If the port should be closed, ask
the grid company to deactivate it. If a key has sat in a file that others have
had access to, ask the grid company for a new one.
