The meter's own port can give you readings several times a minute, so you can
see what a hob or a heat pump draws while it happens. The industry standard
requires the port to send at least every 10 seconds. This guide takes you from
the meter's port to the Home Assistant Energy dashboard.

You get a live power value and a cumulative energy counter that Energy can use.
You need a meter with an open port, a reader, an ESP32 or another reader that
Home Assistant can talk to, and some patience with the grid company; Flow Elnet
states 1 to 2 weeks. Home Assistant must already be running; see
[Getting Started](/en/home-assistant/kom-godt-i-gang/) if not.

## What the HAN port is

The port has different names depending on country and source. The Green Power
Denmark [industry standard](https://greenpowerdenmark.dk/files/media/document/Branchestandard-for-fjernaflaeste-elmaaleres-eksterne-port.pdf)
lists H1 port, P1 port (Dutch) and HAN port (Norwegian) as names for the same
customer interface, and uses H1 itself. Here we call it the HAN port.

Under [section 7 of the Danish meter order](https://ens.dk/media/447/download)
you have the right to connect external devices to the remotely read meter using
open standards and to take out consumption data continuously. The Danish Energy
Agency writes that the grid company must hand out the keys that fit the software
in the meter that gives you access to your data. The physical design and the
specific standard are chosen by the grid company, so your meter may differ from
the examples here.

The standard recommends that the port only sends data to you, at least every
10 seconds, and that it can be encrypted. It also recommends that the port is
off by default and that first activation costs you nothing. That is an
industry norm, not a promise from your grid company; read their own terms. The
figures are also statistical, not billing values.

<Aside type="caution" title="Port and module are not the same on every meter">
At some grid companies the port sits under the meter's cover and you must buy a
module that fits the meter type. Ask the module supplier which key type and
connector it uses before you order anything.
</Aside>

## Choose a reader

| Solution | Suits | You maintain |
|---|---|---|
| Ready-made reader with a Home Assistant integration | You want it simple | The maker's firmware and support |
| ESP32 with ESPHome and an adapter | You want to build and control everything | Config file, key and ESPHome updates |
| Home Assistant's DSMR integration | Dutch, Belgian, Luxembourg or Swedish DSMR meters | Not documented for Denmark |

Home Assistant's [DSMR integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/dsmr/)
does not mention Denmark, and Swedish meters with a HAN port are not supported.
Check a ready-made reader's own documentation for your meter type and grid
company before you buy it; this guide cannot know which ones work for you.

The DIY route uses the ESPHome
[DLMS Meter](https://esphome.io/components/sensor/dlms_meter/) component. It only
listens and decodes the packets the meter's port sends. It documents a setup
for Kamstrup Omnipower and mentions Landis+Gyr and others. ESPHome recommends an
ESP32 because an ESP8266 is not suited to large packets, and it requires an
adapter (for example M-Bus to UART) between the meter and the ESP.

## Step by step

<Steps>
1. **Find your meter type and your grid company**

   The grid company owns the meter and delivers power to the house; it is not
   necessarily the company you buy electricity from. Find the grid company's
   page about meters. Flow Elnet, for example, has a
   [page on getting data](https://flow-elnet.dk/hent-data-fra-din-maaler) per
   meter type.

2. **Ask for the port to be opened and for keys**

   Use the grid company's form. Radius Elnet describes that you
   [order an encryption key](https://radiuselnet.dk/din-tilslutning/bestil-krypteringsnoegle/)
   once you have bought a module, and that you receive one or two codes by
   email. Flow Elnet writes that the port on newer meters is closed from the
   factory and that they open it or email a code, which according to the page
   takes 1 to 2 weeks. Radius' form asks for things such as the customer
   (aftagenummer) and meter numbers. Prices and deadlines differ; check with
   your own company.

3. **Connect the reader**

   Fit the module following the supplier's instructions. For ESPHome you wire
   the adapter to a UART on your ESP32.

4. **Configure the reader**

   You need the baud rate and connector from the supplier; ESPHome says the UART
   settings depend on your meter. Keep the key in a private configuration file,
   never in a public repository.

5. **Add the device in Home Assistant**

   Go to **Settings → Devices & services**. A discovered ESPHome device appears
   as a card. Otherwise choose **Add Integration → ESPHome** and enter the host
   name or IP address. See the
   [ESPHome integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/esphome/).

6. **Add the meter to Energy**

   Under **Settings → Dashboards → Energy** choose **Add grid connection** and
   select the energy sensor for consumption from the grid and, if you have it,
   for export. Do not select a power sensor as energy without following
   [Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/).
</Steps>

### ESPHome example

This follows ESPHome's own examples. Everything in capitals must be replaced;
the key, pins and baud rate come from the grid company and the module supplier.

```yaml
uart:
  id: uart_dlms_bus
  rx_pin: GPIOXX
  baud_rate: BAUD_RATE_FROM_SUPPLIER

dlms_meter:
  id: dlms_meter_hub
  uart_id: uart_dlms_bus
  decryption_key: "YOUR_32_CHAR_HEX_KEY"
  # auth_key: "YOUR_AUTH_KEY"   # only if your grid company requires it

sensor:
  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.1.8.0.255"
    name: "Energy from grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "kWh"
    accuracy_decimals: 3
    device_class: energy
    state_class: total_increasing

  - platform: dlms_meter
    dlms_meter_id: dlms_meter_hub
    obis_code: "1.0.1.7.0.255"
    name: "Power from grid"
    unit_of_measurement: "kW"   # the standard specifies kW; check against the log
    device_class: power
    state_class: measurement
```

ESPHome shows `1.0.1.8.0.255` as energy consumed and `1.0.1.7.0.255` as active
power taken from the grid. The industry standard's table also lists export (2.8.0
and 2.7.0). Write the export code in the same format and check it in the log
before you trust it. The standard specifies power in kW; set the unit to match
what the log shows. Keep `logger` at `VERBOSE` only during setup.

ESPHome states that the old predefined keys such as `voltage_l1` and the
`provider` option are deprecated and removed in 2026.11.0. Use `obis_code`.

## Test it

1. Open the new device in Home Assistant. Power and energy should update every
   few seconds, and neither may show `unavailable`.
2. Switch on a known load, for example a 2 kW kettle. Power should rise by about
   that amount and fall when you switch it off.
3. Compare with the meter display: note the kWh counter in Home Assistant and on
   the display at the same moment, wait an hour or a day, and compare the
   change. Small differences are normal: the port sends statistical values, and
   the grid company's billing is not the same measurement.
4. Under **Settings → Tools → Statistics**, check that the energy sensor has
   long-term statistics and that the Energy page draws the consumption the next
   hour.

## When no data arrives

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing in the log | The port is still closed or the module is not activated | Ask the grid company whether the opening is done |
| Packets arrive but values are missing | Wrong key or missing `auth_key` | Check that you use the key type the module requires |
| Garbled or empty values | Wrong baud rate, parity or pin | Fix the UART per the supplier; set `logger` to `VERBOSE` temporarily |
| Large packets are lost | ESP8266 or too small a buffer | Use an ESP32; the UART page mentions `rx_buffer_size` for large payloads |
| The sensor cannot be chosen in Energy | `device_class` or `state_class` missing | Set them as in the example and wait for statistics |
| Data suddenly stopped | The port may have been deactivated, for example at a change of customer | Ask the grid company to restore the port |

If consumption is doubled or negative in Energy, see
[troubleshooting in Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/).

## Undo

Remove the grid connection under **Settings → Dashboards → Energy**, delete the
ESPHome device under **Devices & services**, and take the reader out of the
port. History in Home Assistant is unchanged. If the port should be closed,
ask the grid company to deactivate it; if a key has sat in a file that has been
shared, ask for it to be replaced.
