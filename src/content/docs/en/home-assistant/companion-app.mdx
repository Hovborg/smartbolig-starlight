The Companion app is Home Assistant's own app for Android and iPhone. It shows
your dashboard, but it also turns the phone into a device in Home Assistant: the
phone can send sensors and its location to the server and receive notifications.
This guide takes you from installation to a first test message and explains what
the app sends and what that means for battery and privacy.

![The phone with the Companion app talks to the Home Assistant server on the local address at home and the external address away. Sensors and location go up to the server; the dashboard comes down to the phone. Notifications normally go through Apple's or Google's push service.](../../../../assets/guides/companion-app/overview-en.svg)

## What you need

- A Home Assistant server running version 0.104.0 or later.
- Android 6 or later, or iOS 16.4 or later.
- The server's address and a login for it.

Get the app from [companion.home-assistant.io/download](https://companion.home-assistant.io/download).

## Install and connect the app

<Steps>
1. **Open the app** and choose **Connect to my Home Assistant server** on the
   welcome screen.

2. **Pick the server.** The app searches your network. If it finds one, it
   offers to connect. If it finds none, or you are not at home, choose
   **Enter address manually** and type the address.

3. **Log in** with your Home Assistant login. Do not use Trusted Networks for
   the app, or it will only work on the trusted network.

4. **Name the device.** The name appears in Home Assistant and is used for the
   device's entities, so pick something recognisable, for example `My phone`.

5. **Choose location.** **Share my location** lets automations know whether you
   are home and is needed for the most secure connection. On Android you grant
   **Allow all the time** and **Nearby devices**; on iPhone you choose
   **Allow While Using the App** and then **Allow always**. **Do not share my
   location** is also a valid choice, but on iPhone none of the sensors will
   work if location is turned off.

6. **Choose a connection security level** if the address is not encrypted, for
   example a local IP address. **Most secure** uses the unencrypted address only
   on your home network. **Less secure** uses it on any network and is not
   recommended.

7. **Allow notifications** when the app asks. On iPhone it also asks about
   *critical notifications*, which sound even in silent mode.
</Steps>

At the end the app opens your dashboard.

## Local and remote address

At home the app can use the server's local address. Away from home it needs an
external address, and the connection must be encrypted: an HTTPS address or a
VPN. The recommended route is Home Assistant Cloud. It is all covered in
[Secure remote access to Home Assistant](/en/sikkerhed/fjernadgang/).

The app picks the address from the name of the Wi-Fi network it is on. Under
**Settings → Companion app → [your server]** you find **Internal URL**, where
you enter your home network's Wi-Fi names, and **Connection security level**.
The app can only read the Wi-Fi name with location access: **Always** and **Precise** on iPhone,
**Allow all the time** on Android. Without it, **Most secure** uses only the
external HTTPS address.

## What the app sends

**Sensors.** On Android most are off by default. The exceptions are battery
level, battery state and charger type, plus the sensors you granted permission
to during setup. On iPhone not all sensors are on by default either. On Android
they update every 15 minutes by default; on iPhone they update in the background
at a rate iOS decides.

**Device tracker.** After the first launch the app creates a `device_tracker`
entity, typically named after the device ID. You find it on the Mobile App
integration and can rename it.

**Zones.** On iPhone the app sets up geofences for all your zones itself and
reports when you enter and leave. On Android you need to switch on the
**Location Zone** sensor.

## Permissions and what they are used for

| Permission | Used for |
|---|---|
| Location (Android: *Allow all the time*, iPhone: *Always*) | Device tracker, zones and choosing the address from the Wi-Fi network |
| Nearby devices (Android) | Background location updates |
| Notifications | Messages from Home Assistant; critical messages on iPhone |
| Motion (iPhone) | Activity and step counting |
| Camera (iPhone) | The camera motion and camera stream sensors |
| Activity recognition (Android) | Activity sensors and the step counter |
| Phone (Android) | The phone state sensor |
| Notification access (Android) | Notification sensors |

You do not have to grant them all. Grant a permission when you switch on the
sensor that needs it. Apple Health sensors are off until you turn them on.

## Switch sensors on and off

- **iPhone:** **Settings → Companion app → Sensors.** With several servers,
  choose the server under **Servers** first.
- **Android:** **Settings → Companion App → Manage Sensors**, then pick a
  sensor. The frequency is instead under **Settings → Companion App → Sensor Update
  Frequency**.

To stop an iPhone sending any sensors to a server, set **Sensors Sent** to
**None** under **Privacy** on that server.

Under **Location Sent** (iPhone: under the server's **Privacy**; Android: under
**Manage Sensors → Background Location**) you choose how much location the
server gets:
**Exact** sends GPS coordinates, **Zone Name Only** sends just the zone name or
`not_home`, and **Never** (iPhone) or **Disabled** (Android) sends nothing. To
stop tracking without removing the permission, you can disable the
`device_tracker` entity in the entity registry.

Battery: the **High accuracy mode** option on the Android **Background
Location** sensor uses GPS continuously and drains the battery faster. The
notification sensor on Android can use a lot of battery without an allow list,
and the **BLE Transmitter** sensor can affect battery too. Switch on only what you use. According to the documentation the
location goes from the phone to your server, directly or through Home Assistant
Cloud, and not through other servers.

## Send a test notification

<Steps>
1. Go to **Settings → Tools → Actions**. The page was called *Developer tools*
   before Home Assistant 2026.8 and moved into Settings in 2026.2.
2. Pick an action that starts with `notify.mobile_app_`, followed by your
   phone's ID. The ID is based on the device name, with spaces and special
   characters replaced by underscores.
3. Enter a message in YAML mode, and change the name to your own phone:

   ```yaml
   action: notify.mobile_app_my_phone
   data:
     message: "Hello World"
   ```

4. Select **Perform action**. The message should appear on the phone. If the
   action is missing, restart Home Assistant.
</Steps>

Notification groups for several phones are
in [Send one message to several phones](/en/home-assistant/mobilnotifikationer/).

## Troubleshooting

**Sensors do not update.** On iPhone, location must be set to *Always*, and iOS
limits updates in the background. For battery level or charging, you can make an
automation in Shortcuts with the **Update Sensors** action. On Android a sensor
only appears once it has received a state, so wait for a change and check that
its permissions are granted.

**Location is stuck.** Set location to *Allow all the time* (Android) or
*Always* (iPhone). On Android also turn on GPS and allow precise location, and
**Background location**, **Location zone** and **Single accurate location** must
be switched on under **Manage Sensors**. The app must not be restricted by
battery optimisation. If your `person` entity is stuck, remove tracked
devices that just stay at home or are no longer used.

**Notifications do not arrive.** First try the test message above. If it
arrives, your original message is probably malformed. Each app can receive at
most 500 notifications a day, and the count resets at midnight UTC. Check that
the phone allows notifications from Home Assistant; on Android that includes the
channel you use.

**The app cannot connect when you are away.** If Home Assistant Cloud is on
without Remote UI, turn on Remote UI, or turn **Connect Via Cloud** off and enter
an encrypted external address. See
[Secure remote access](/en/sikkerhed/fjernadgang/).
