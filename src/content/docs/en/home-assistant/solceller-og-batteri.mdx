If you have solar panels or a home battery, you rarely know how much you use
yourself, how much goes to the grid, and when the battery is working. This guide
gets production, grid and battery into Home Assistant. You then use the numbers
in a simple automation that shifts consumption to hours with surplus.

You get an Energy dashboard with production, grid and battery, a solar forecast,
and an automation for one load that may switch on and off without you. You need
energy sensors from your inverter or from a meter. Energy must already work
with the grid; see [Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/) and,
if you read live from the meter, [your meter via the HAN port](/en/home-assistant/elmaaler-han-port/).

The safety rules in this guide are the Danish ones.

## Choose how the data gets in

Home Assistant's [solar panels page](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/energy/solar-panels/)
names two routes to production data: an integration that reads the inverter's
API, and CT clamps that measure the current in a wire. The page points to the
Shelly EM as an off-the-shelf solution and to ESPHome for a DIY build. The
[battery page](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/energy/battery/) similarly
names an integration (the example is Tesla Powerwall) or CT clamps.

| Source | Suits | You maintain |
|---|---|---|
| Integration for your inverter or battery | The manufacturer has an integration | The integration's status and login |
| Shelly EM or another meter with CT clamps | The inverter has no usable integration | The meter, its placement and firmware |
| ESPHome with a CT sensor | You want to build it yourself | Configuration and calibration |

<Aside type="caution" title="Work in the electrical board requires authorisation">
CT clamps and the meters for them are fitted in the electrical board. Home
Assistant writes that this requires opening the board and may require a
licensed professional in some regions. In Denmark, according to the Danish
Safety Technology Authority, you may not do electrical work in the meter board
or the consumer unit yourself. Have an authorised electrical contractor fit the
meter and the clamps. Connecting the solar installation itself in the board
also requires authorisation. You may do the setup in Home Assistant yourself.
</Aside>

## Step by step

<Steps>
1. **Find the sensors**

   Use **Settings → Tools → States** to find the sensors from your integration.
   You need an energy sensor in kWh or Wh for each of these: consumption from
   the grid, export to the grid, solar production, energy into the battery and
   energy out of the battery. The requirements for the sensors are in Home
   Assistant's [Energy FAQ](https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/energy/faq/).

2. **Add the grid first**

   Go to **Settings → Dashboards → Energy**. Under **Electricity grid** choose
   **Add grid connection** and select the sensors for consumption and export.
   See [Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/).

3. **Add solar panels**

   Under **Solar panels** choose **Add solar production**. Select the
   inverter's total production counter. Do not use grid export as production;
   the power you used yourself would be missing.

4. **Add the battery**

   Under **Home battery storage** choose **Add battery system**. Select one
   sensor for energy into the battery and one for energy out. State of charge
   in percent is useful for operation but does not replace the two energy
   sensors. Check the direction: when the battery charges, the counter for
   energy in should rise.

5. **Turn on the solar forecast**

   Add the [Forecast.Solar](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/forecast_solar/)
   integration under **Settings → Devices & services → Add integration**. You
   enter the tilt (0 is horizontal, 90 is vertical), the azimuth (0 is north,
   90 is east, 180 is south, 270 is west) and the installation's total power in
   watt-peak. Then go to Energy. Under **Solar panels**, open your solar
   production, turn on **Solar production forecast**, select Forecast.Solar and
   save.

6. **Create a surplus sensor**

   Go to **Settings → Devices & services → Helpers → Create helper** and pick
   the Threshold type. Choose a sensor that shows the power you send to the
   grid as a positive number. Fill in only **Upper limit** and **Hysteresis**.
   The helper then turns on when the value is above the limit plus the
   hysteresis, and turns off when it is below the limit minus the hysteresis.
   See the [Threshold integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/threshold/).

   Choose the numbers from the load. When the load switches on, export drops by
   the load's power. The switch-on point must therefore be above the load's
   power, and the switch-off point must be just above zero. Otherwise the load
   switches on and off in a loop.

   Example for a load of about 2000 W: upper limit 1300 and hysteresis 1100.
   The helper turns on above 2400 W of export and turns off below 200 W. The
   limits are in the sensor's own unit. If the sensor shows kW, the limits must
   be in kW too. If you have a battery, the inverter's settings decide whether
   surplus goes to the battery or to the grid first. See when export actually
   appears before you choose limits.

   We call the helper `binary_sensor.solcelle_overskud` here.

7. **Create the automation**

   Go to **Settings → Automations & scenes**, choose **Create automation** and
   then **Create new automation**. Open the three-dot menu and choose **Edit in
   YAML**. Paste the example below and replace the entity IDs with your own.
</Steps>

### Automation: run a load on surplus

<Aside type="caution" title="The load must be connected lawfully">
You may start using a smart plug that you put into a socket yourself. Check in
the manufacturer's data that it is rated for the appliance's power. If the load
is permanently wired without a plug, the Danish Safety Technology Authority
says you may not connect or disconnect it yourself. Have an authorised
electrical contractor fit the control.
</Aside>

The example uses state triggers with `for:`, so a passing cloud does not switch
the load on and off all the time. Choose a load that tolerates being
interrupted, for example a hot water tank with its own thermostat.

```yaml title="Automation: Load follows solar surplus"
alias: "Load follows solar surplus"
description: "Switches a load on at steady surplus and off again"
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.solcelle_overskud
    to: "on"
    for: "00:05:00"
    id: surplus_on

  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.solcelle_overskud
    to: "off"
    for: "00:05:00"
    id: surplus_off

  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.solcelle_overskud
    to:
      - "unavailable"
      - "unknown"
    for: "00:10:00"
    id: sensor_lost

conditions: []

actions:
  - choose:
      - conditions:
          - condition: trigger
            id: surplus_on
        sequence:
          - action: switch.turn_on
            target:
              entity_id: switch.varmtvandsbeholder

      - conditions:
          - condition: trigger
            id:
              - surplus_off
              - sensor_lost
        sequence:
          - action: switch.turn_off
            target:
              entity_id: switch.varmtvandsbeholder

mode: restart
```

How it works:

- The first two triggers switch on and off when the helper has been `on` or
  `off` for five minutes.
- The third trigger switches the load off if the helper has been `unavailable`
  or `unknown` for ten minutes. The Threshold helper becomes `unknown` when the
  source sensor is `unavailable`, `unknown` or does not give a number.
- The trigger does not catch a sensor that is stuck on an old value. That is
  one more reason to choose a load that tolerates staying on.
- Home Assistant states that the `for:` timer resets when Home Assistant
  restarts or automations reload.
- If you switch the load off by hand, the automation only switches it on again
  the next time the helper changes to `on`.

To switch on only when the battery is reasonably full, add a condition to the
first branch. According to Home Assistant, a
[numeric_state condition](https://www.home-assistant.io/conditions/numeric_state/)
does not pass if the sensor is `unavailable` or `unknown`:

```yaml
          - condition: numeric_state
            entity_id: sensor.batteri_ladetilstand
            above: 80
```

To shift use by price instead, see the
[electricity price integration](/en/home-assistant/elpris-integration/) and
[EV preheating](/en/home-assistant/elbil-opvarmning/). For an overview of the
ways to measure, see [energy management](/en/produkter/energistyring/).

## Test it

1. In Energy the grid, solar panels and battery should show numbers. Home
   Assistant itself says it can take up to 2 hours before new data shows.
   Compare today's production with the inverter's own display; large deviations
   point to the wrong sensor or unit.
2. Watch the export sensor under **Settings → Tools → States** on a sunny day.
   Switch on a known load, for example a kettle. Export should drop by about
   the load's power.
3. Open the automation and choose **Traces**. Check that it ran the right
   branch; see [Activity and Traces](/en/home-assistant/aktivitet-og-spor/).
   **Run actions** starts the automation without a trigger, so neither branch
   runs. Test with a real change.
4. Check that the load actually switched on and off, not only that the state
   changed in Home Assistant.
5. Compare the forecast with actual production for a few days. Forecast.Solar
   itself says the forecast is an estimate.

## When it does not work

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| The sensor cannot be chosen in Energy | Wrong unit, `device_class` or `state_class` | See [Energy Dashboard](/en/home-assistant/energy-dashboard/) |
| Production or consumption is counted twice | The same energy is selected both as a total and as a sub-total | Remove one |
| The battery gives wrong numbers | One sensor covers both charging and discharging | Use two energy sensors, one for in and one for out |
| The forecast is empty | Forecast.Solar is not selected under Solar panels | Turn on the forecast in step 5 |
| The load switches on and off constantly | The switch-on point is below the load's power, or the hysteresis is too small | Set the limits as in step 6, or lengthen `for:` |
| The load stays on after a restart | The `for:` timer was reset, and the helper has not changed since | Switch it off by hand, or add a [Home Assistant start trigger](https://www.home-assistant.io/triggers/homeassistant/) |

## Undo

Disable the automation and switch the load on or off by hand. Delete the
Threshold helper under **Helpers**, and remove solar panels or battery under
**Settings → Dashboards → Energy**. This does not change the sensors' history.
