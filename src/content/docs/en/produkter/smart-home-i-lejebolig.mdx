In a rented flat you cannot just drill, replace or rebuild. Fortunately a good
smart home rarely needs it. With devices that plug into a socket, screw into a
fitting or sit on a shelf, you can get control, sensors and automations that you
can pack down on the day you move.

This guide gives you the overview: what you can do, what to leave alone, and how
to make moving easy. It recommends no brands or shops; it describes device types
and protocols and points on to the site's detailed guides. The rules described
here are Danish.

## What you get

- A smart home made only of devices you can take with you.
- Control of lights and appliances, sensors and heating.
- A clear picture of where the fixed installation begins.
- A plan for moving everything without starting over.

## What you need

- A Home Assistant installation you can move, for example on a small computer.
  See [Getting started](/en/home-assistant/kom-godt-i-gang/).
- Your lease, so you know what applies to changes and to restoring the flat.
- A plan for how the devices talk to each other: Zigbee, Wi-Fi or Matter over
  Thread.

## What you may do and what to leave alone

The rules on electrical work belong to the Danish Safety Technology Authority
(Sikkerhedsstyrelsen), which has been part of the Danish Business Authority
(Erhvervsstyrelsen) since January 2026. The guidance on sik.dk is a common
starting point, not a decision about your home.

| You can | Leave alone |
|---|---|
| Put a smart plug in an existing socket | Putting up a new switch or socket where there has not been one before |
| Screw smart bulbs into an existing fitting | Working in the meter, panel or fixed wiring |
| Place battery sensors and wireless buttons | Connecting or disconnecting anything without a plug, for example hard-wired white goods |
| Connect appliances that have a plug | Fitting modules behind switches or in lamp outlets |

Sikkerhedsstyrelsen writes that a private person or a company without
authorisation may never work on the fixed electrical installations, and that
this also applies to white goods. You may only connect or disconnect an
electrical product if it can be done with a plug into or out of a socket.
Otherwise the work is for an authorised company. See
[Which electrical products may you connect or disconnect yourself?](https://www.sik.dk/privat/gor-det-sikkert/el/brug-elprodukter-sikkert/hvilke-elprodukter-ma-du-selv-slutte-eller-koble)
(in Danish).

The same authority writes that it is not permitted to put up a new switch or
socket in the fixed installation if there has not been one in the same place
before. You may never establish a socket yourself. See
[How to replace a socket](https://www.sik.dk/privat/goer-det-sikkert/el/goer-det-selv/saadan-skifter-du-stikkontakt)
(in Danish).

What anyone may do themselves is listed in
[Executive Order no. 560 of 30 May 2017](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/560)
(in Danish). For electrical work it mentions, among other things, replacing
switches and sockets for at most 250 V on certain conditions and putting up and
taking down light fittings. In its guidance on replacing a socket,
Sikkerhedsstyrelsen writes that the condition is that you know how to do it
correctly. In a rented home the owner is responsible for the fixed electrical
installations, so ask your landlord before you replace anything. See
[Which electrical work may you do without an authorisation?](https://www.sik.dk/privat/goer-det-sikkert/el/goer-det-selv/hvilket-elarbejde-maa-du-lave-uden-autorisation)
(in Danish).

<Aside type="caution" title="Modules behind switches">
A smart relay fitted behind a switch or in a lamp outlet becomes part of the
fixed installation. Modules of this kind are not mentioned in the executive
order's list of work anyone may do, and the sik.dk pages we have read do not
discuss them. Therefore let an authorised electrician fit them.

Sikkerhedsstyrelsen writes that the owner is responsible for the fixed
electrical installations, also when the home is rented out. So get your
landlord's written yes first. See
[Your responsibility as the owner of rented homes](https://www.sik.dk/privat/goer-det-sikkert/el/el-lejeboliger/dit-ansvar-ejer-lejeboliger)
(in Danish). Otherwise use a smart plug or a bulb.
</Aside>

### The Rent Act: ask your landlord in writing

The following restates what the law says, in our own translation. It is not
legal advice, and we cannot decide what applies to your tenancy.

The Danish Rent Act (lejeloven) applies to the rent of houses or rooms, but not
to social housing (almene boliger) (section 1). The Act says:

- **Section 141:** The tenant may not, without the landlord's consent, make
  changes to the rented property or put up fittings or objects other than those
  mentioned in sections 142-145.
- **Section 142(1):** The tenant has the right to make customary installations
  in the rented property unless the landlord can show that the property's
  electrical and drainage capacity is not sufficient for the installation. The
  tenant must notify the landlord before making the installation.
- **Section 142(3):** The tenant is liable for damage caused by the
  installations the tenant has made. The landlord can require the tenant to
  provide security for that liability.
- **Section 147:** Disputes about the tenant's rights and obligations under the
  chapter are decided by the rent tribunal (huslejenævnet).

The Act does not define what a "customary installation" is. So write to your
landlord before you put up anything that is fixed to the home or changes it,
for example a radiator thermostat or anything that must be screwed into the
wall. Describe what you want to fit, and keep the answer. The lease and the
house rules may have their own rules.

The text is from the
[Rent Act](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/341) (Act no. 341 of 22
March 2022, in Danish) with the amending acts Retsinformation showed on 14
November 2026. Always check the current text.

If you live in social housing, the
[Act on the Rent of Social Housing](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2019/928)
(in Danish) applies instead. Its section 35 has the same wording about
customary installations. Ask your housing organisation.

The authorisation rules apply alongside the Rent Act. The right to an
installation does not change who may carry out the electrical work.

If you want to fit a radiator thermostat, change only the thermostat head, not
the valve, and leave the pipes alone. Sikkerhedsstyrelsen's page on plumbing
work does not mention radiator thermostats. It says that you may replace a
radiator, but that district heating can have local rules. So ask your landlord
and, with district heating, also the utility if you are in doubt. See
[Which plumbing work may you do without an authorisation?](https://www.sik.dk/privat/goer-det-sikkert/gas-vand-og-afloeb/hvilket-vvs-arbejde-maa-du-lave-uden-autorisation)
(in Danish).

## How to do it

<Steps>
1. Read the lease, and write to your landlord before you change anything that
   is fixed to the home.
2. Choose a protocol for sensors and buttons. See the section below.
3. Start with lights and plugs that can be put in and taken out again.
4. Add battery sensors for temperature, openings and motion.
5. Add radiator thermostats in the rooms where you want to control heating,
   once your landlord has said yes.
6. Note down the device setup, and take a backup.
</Steps>

### Plugs

A smart plug sits between the wall and the appliance. It can switch on and off,
and some can measure consumption. Choose one rated for the appliance's power,
and compare the markings on the plug and the appliance. Do not use it for
appliances that are hard-wired. See
[Energy management](/en/produkter/energistyring/) on measuring consumption.

### Bulbs

Smart bulbs screw into the existing fitting. If you switch off at the wall
switch, the bulb loses power and can no longer be controlled. A wireless button
lets you control the light without touching the switch. See
[Smart lighting](/en/produkter/smart-belysning/).

### Sensors and buttons

Battery sensors can stand on a shelf or be held with adhesive strips. Check the
lease for requirements about the condition on handover before you stick
anything. See [Zigbee sensors](/en/produkter/zigbee-sensorer/).

### Radiator thermostats

A smart radiator thermostat replaces the manual thermostat head and controls
how far the valve opens. See [Smart thermostats](/en/produkter/smart-termostater/).
Keep the old head so you can put it back when you move.

## Choose a protocol

| Protocol | In short | Read more |
|---|---|---|
| Zigbee | Own radio network with a coordinator; mesh | [Zigbee coordinators](/en/produkter/zigbee-koordinatorer/) |
| Wi-Fi | No extra hub; depends on your network | [Wi-Fi devices](/en/produkter/wifi-enheder/) |
| Matter over Thread | Open standard; needs a Thread border router | [Matter and Thread](/en/home-assistant/thread-matter/) |

For Zigbee you can use either
[Zigbee2MQTT](/en/home-assistant/zigbee2mqtt/) or
[ZHA](/en/home-assistant/zha-enhedshaandtering/). Home Assistant's ZHA
documentation states that a Zigbee device can only be connected to a single
coordinator. If it has been used in another Zigbee system, it must be factory
reset before it can be added.

Matter devices are paired with a QR code or a numeric code. Home Assistant
recommends taking a picture of the code, because you need it again if the
device is reset. Matter devices on Wi-Fi and Thread border routers must be on
the same LAN/VLAN as Home Assistant.

## How to take everything with you

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Take the coordinator" icon="rocket">
    The Zigbee devices are connected to your coordinator. Take both it and the
    Home Assistant computer with you.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Take a backup" icon="open-book">
    See [Backup and security](/en/home-assistant/backup-sikkerhed/). ZHA
    automatically backs up its Zigbee network.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Give Wi-Fi devices the new network" icon="setting">
    If you get a new network name or password, the Wi-Fi devices must be set up
    on the new network. How that is done depends on the device; read the
    manufacturer's instructions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keep the Matter codes" icon="document">
    You need the QR code or the numeric code again if a Matter device is reset.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

If you change to a new Zigbee radio, the ZHA documentation describes a
migration in which devices normally rejoin within an hour. See the
[ZHA documentation](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/zha/). Keep the
thermostat heads and the original bulbs from the flat so you can put them back.

Check the lease for how the flat must be handed over when you move. Remove
adhesive strips carefully, and put the old bulbs and thermostat heads back.

## Test it

1. Switch a smart plug on and off from Home Assistant, and check that the
   appliance follows.
2. Open a door with a sensor, and see that the state changes in Home Assistant.
3. Change the temperature target on a radiator thermostat, and see that the
   valve responds.
4. Cut the power to the Home Assistant computer and the coordinator, restore it,
   and see that the devices come back.
5. Take a backup, and check that you can find it.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A bulb cannot be controlled | The wall switch is off | Leave the switch on; use a wireless button |
| A device cannot be added | It belongs to another system | Reset it following the manufacturer's instructions |
| A Wi-Fi device is gone after the move | New network name or password | Set it up on the new network |
| A Thread device does not respond | No border router nearby | See [Matter and Thread](/en/home-assistant/thread-matter/) |
| A thermostat does not fit the valve | Wrong adapter | See the manufacturer's list of valves |

## How to undo it

Remove the devices in reverse order: thermostats, sensors, bulbs and plugs. Put
the original parts back, and delete the devices in Home Assistant so they do not
show as unavailable. If you have neither drilled nor changed anything, the flat
is as it was.
